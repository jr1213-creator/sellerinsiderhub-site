MARKET / MONETIZATION REVIEW — INTERNAL IMPLEMENTATION RECORD
Reviewed 2026-11-01. No investor material.

Recommendation
Keep the complete site broad enough for product sellers, creators, and service operators, but make each article answer one identifiable job. The strongest page sequence is a usable tutorial, a scenario-based comparison, and a tool profile whose plan and limits match that tutorial. The reader should leave with a draft, queue, booking test, cost worksheet, or decision they can inspect.

Evidence
The Chamber reports 66% AI use and 41% use of six or more technology platforms in its 2026 small-business survey. Census BTOS reports a much lower 17–20% usage range in a different population and time window. These support implementation relevance, not a paid-software market-size claim. Etsy’s filing reports a predominantly solo seller base and substantial multichannel activity. Public search returned dense comparison coverage, official tutorials, and vendor-written alternatives pages. The detailed URLs and dates are in data/research/market-research.json.

Objection: high-ticket products will make the site profitable quickly.
Response: commission size cannot compensate for poor reader fit, insufficient qualified visitors, missing program approval, or conversion and payout delays. A solo seller may need a free review link or a $6 research tool; a multi-location operator may justify a quoted suite. Recommend the cheapest workable scope for the actual job. No revenue forecast is supported by the current evidence.

Objection: a larger AI-generated content inventory is differentiation.
Response: observed results already contain many tool directories and scenario-based comparisons. Original coherent examples, current source reconciliation, copyable artifacts, and tested acceptance criteria are stronger editorial choices. Do not invent hands-on testing, authorship, screenshots, or measured savings to appear established.

Objection: market research requires shrinking the site to one niche.
Response: no scope reduction is needed. Use complete business hubs with different jobs and specific cross-links. Prioritization controls homepage prominence and depth, while the full coverage remains available. A broad navigation structure must not make every tool appear suitable for every business.

Alternatives considered
1. Directory-first publication: easy to expand, but observed competition and low practical distinction make it a weak lead experience.
2. High-ticket-first rankings: potentially attractive commission headlines, but poor fit for many solo sellers and weak editorial trust.
3. Workflow-led publication with documented buying guidance: chosen because the reader receives a concrete artifact and the software decision follows naturally.

Decisions applied
47 tool profiles now have specific audience fit, practical exclusions, documented economics and limits, a unique illustrative business task, a verification check, and two reasoned alternatives. Prices distinguish monthly payment from annual commitment. Free-trial allowances are separated from continuing free capacity. Source conflicts are disclosed. Existing unverified affiliate metadata was preserved rather than fabricated.

Measurement after release
Use observed page visits, completed tool interactions, voluntary subscriptions, relevant outbound clicks, approved referrals, reversals, and actual payouts to evaluate the model. Keep each stage separate. Do not equate a signup or click with revenue, and do not use a hypothetical conversion rate as an operating result. Revisit topic priority using real reader behavior when it exists.
