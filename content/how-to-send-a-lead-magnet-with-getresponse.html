01 / Make the promised resource

Start with a small PDF that solves one problem.

For a consultant, a useful lead magnet demonstrates how you think before asking someone to book. Our example is a one-page Website Enquiry Check for Local Service Businesses. Its job is to help an owner find a confusing or broken contact path. It does not promise more leads.

Completed example: the PDF content

Website Enquiry Check for Local Service Businesses
A practical checklist from Maple Street Studio. Open your homepage on a phone and complete these seven checks.

  1. The offer: Can a new visitor identify your service and service area without opening another page? Write the clearest sentence you can: “We provide [service] for [customer] in [area].”
  2. The next step: Find your main contact button. Does its label describe what happens next, such as “Request a quote,” rather than “Learn more”?
  3. The mobile path: Tap the button with one hand. Check that menus, banners, and popups do not hide the form or phone number.
  4. The useful details: Confirm that your service area, opening hours, and contact information are accurate.
  5. The evidence: Find one real project, relevant qualification, or customer comment you have permission to publish. Remove claims you cannot support.
  6. The form: Submit a test enquiry using your own address. Confirm that the message reaches the monitored inbox and the page explains what happens next.
  7. The response: Check who receives enquiries and what response time you can actually meet. Publish that expectation only if your business can maintain it.

Your next action: Mark each item Clear, Needs work, or Not applicable. Fix one confusing sentence or broken step first. Record the date and test again. This checklist diagnoses the contact path; it cannot predict your traffic, enquiries, or sales.

Put your business name, a contact route, and a version date in the document. Export a readable PDF with selectable text. Open it on a phone before uploading. A polished cover matters less than a resource readers can use.

02 / Prepare the sender and audience

Create one list and authenticate your sender.

  1. Open Contacts → Lists → Create list. Give this sequence a recognizable name, such as Website enquiry checklist.
  2. Open Profile → Emails and domains. Add and verify a sender address on a domain you control, such as your business-domain inbox.
  3. Follow GetResponse’s domain authentication instructions for DKIM and DMARC. Use the records supplied for your domain or its supported automatic setup. Preserve existing DNS settings used by other services.
  4. Select a reply-to address someone monitors. Confirm your business details and the postal address used in the email footer.

An address verification email and domain authentication are separate steps. The first confirms access to the inbox; the second establishes the domain’s sending configuration. Check the displayed authentication status before promoting the signup page. This setup improves the sending foundation but does not guarantee inbox placement.

Sources: create a list, domain authentication, sender status, and footer requirements.

03 / Host the download

Upload the PDF and test its public URL.

  1. Go to Profile → Files and Images, then your uploaded files under My Files.
  2. Choose Add files, select the PDF, and place it in a clearly named folder.
  3. When the upload completes, open the file’s action menu and select Copy URL.
  4. Paste the URL into a private browser window. Check that the correct document opens without an account login.

GetResponse documents PDF support, 2GB of storage, and a maximum of 50MB per file. Keep this short checklist comfortably smaller. Save the exact URL in your campaign notes; it becomes the email’s download destination.

A linked file can be forwarded. Treat it as a public resource, not a secure client portal. If you later upload a replacement, verify the URL again and update every email that needs the new file.

Source: Files and Images, supported formats, limits, and Copy URL.

04 / Build delivery first

Create the Day 0 autoresponder before publishing the page.

Open Autoresponders → Create autoresponder, choose By myself, and give the message an internal name such as Checklist 01 — delivery. Select your checklist list, assign Day 0, and choose same time signed up. Allow sending on every day of the week so a weekend signup does not wait until Monday.

Select your verified From and reply-to addresses. Add the subject, choose Design message, and put the PDF URL behind the download button and a plain text link. Use Save and Publish when ready. A saved draft alone will not deliver the resource.

05 / Connect the signup page

Make the page’s promise match the email sequence.

Go to Landing pages → Create landing page → Use a ready-made template. Choose a simple template in the current builder. If your account shows separate New and Legacy areas, use the current/New route. In the editor, use a Signup form; a Contact form is intended for enquiries and does not serve the same list-building purpose.

Landing-page copy to adapt

Headline: Can a new visitor find the next step on your website?

Introduction: Get a one-page checklist for checking your service description, mobile contact button, enquiry form, and response expectations.

What is inside: Seven practical checks, a simple way to record what needs work, and one clear next action.

Form fields: Email address; first name only if you will use it.

Button: Send the checklist and two follow-up emails

Expectation beside the form: Join Maple Street Studio’s three-email website enquiry series. The first email contains the PDF; the next two share a practical tip and an invitation to request a website review. Unsubscribe whenever you choose. Read our Privacy Policy.

Success message with double opt-in: Check your inbox to confirm your subscription. After you confirm, we will email your checklist. If the confirmation is missing, check spam or promotions and make sure you entered the right address.

Replace the privacy-policy text with a working link and make the stated email commitment accurate. Where you collect consent through a field, use an unticked, clear choice for this communication purpose. Do not silently turn this three-email request into an ongoing newsletter subscription. GetResponse’s consent tools help record choices; the text still needs to match your audience and actual practices.

Open the page’s Actions → Edit settings → Subscription settings. Select the checklist list, enable Add contacts to an autoresponder cycle, and start at Day 0. Enable Send an opt-in confirmation email for this example. Page opt-in settings take priority over list settings. Publish the page after checking its address and mobile layout.

With double opt-in, the confirmation message comes first; your PDF delivery email follows confirmation. Check the confirmation email’s sender and wording too. Do not tell an unconfirmed subscriber that the PDF has already been sent.

Sources: current versus legacy page creation, form types, subscription settings, opt-in precedence, and consent fields.

06 / Continue the conversation

Add the two follow-ups to the same list.

Create two more autoresponders for the checklist list, assigned to Day 2 and Day 5. These are days in the same cycle, not two days and then five additional days. The spacing is an editorial starting point, not a GetResponse requirement. Publish each message and check the schedule in your account.

07 / Verify before sharing

Test the whole signup journey with a new address.

An editor preview checks the message’s appearance. A real test subscription checks whether the page, confirmation, list, and cycle are connected. Use an address you control that is not already on this list.

  • Open the published page on a phone and submit the form.
  • Confirm the success message describes the next step accurately.
  • Receive and complete the double opt-in confirmation.
  • Check that the contact appears in the intended list at the intended cycle start.
  • Receive the Day 0 email; open both PDF links and verify the correct version.
  • Reply and confirm that the monitored inbox receives your response.
  • Check all three messages for placeholders, incorrect business details, and readable footers.
  • Let the test contact complete Days 2 and 5 before treating the full sequence as verified. Test unsubscribe separately with another address you control.

Do not repeatedly submit an existing subscriber to prove delivery. The page can report that the contact is already subscribed; that is different from testing a new contact entering Day 0.

Source: existing-contact behavior.

Measure signups, download clicks, and enquiries.

Record a date range and distinguish the stages: measured page visitors, confirmed subscribers, delivery-email recipients, PDF-link clickers, replies, and genuine service enquiries. A PDF-link click shows an attempt to access the resource, not proof that someone read it. An enquiry is not a sale.

For your own worksheet, calculate confirmed subscribers ÷ measured unique visitors and label it with that exact definition. Separately record unique PDF-link clickers ÷ delivered delivery emails when those counts are available. Do not mix total clicks with unique people or compare different date windows.

GetResponse’s page statistics depend on analytics cookies and the statistics setting. Open tracking can also be distorted by privacy features such as Apple Mail Privacy Protection. Treat opens as directional context, then inspect clicks, replies, and business outcomes. Small samples make percentage swings noisy; change one practical problem at a time.

Sources: page measurement settings, email analytics, and open-tracking limitations. The worksheet ratios above are our explicit working definitions, not claims about every dashboard label.

Frequently asked questions

Can I send the PDF as an attachment?

This tutorial uses a hosted link so readers can open the file without carrying an attachment in each message. GetResponse’s Files and Images documentation supports that approach. You do not need an attachment feature to complete this setup.

Do I need a separate download page?

No. The delivery email can link directly to the hosted PDF. A separate page can add context, but it is another asset to maintain and test.

Will a Free account keep sending the lead magnet?

Not through these autoresponders after the premium trial ends. Confirm an eligible paid plan before depending on the sequence for ongoing delivery.

Can I add existing clients to it?

Do not treat an existing business relationship as evidence that someone requested this sequence. Invite interested people to the signup page and honor their communication choices.

What should I do first?

Finish the PDF, authenticate the sender, and verify one complete signup before promoting the page. A small working sequence gives you something concrete to improve.
