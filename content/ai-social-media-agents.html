01 / Choose your setup

Start with the job you want the system to do.

A scheduler sends content at a chosen time. An automation follows a rule when something happens. An AI agent uses instructions and available tools to decide how to carry out a task. A well-designed system can use all three. Naming a chatbot “Social Media Manager” does not, by itself, connect an account or create a background service.

Chat-led route: prepare your source pack, set up Buffer, verify your chat connection can create a draft, then add a supported reply workflow. The complete instructions follow below.

This selector uses fixed editorial guidance. It does not connect to your accounts or send your choice anywhere.

02 / Prepare once

Collect the facts your AI needs to represent you well.

Create a document named Social Media Source Pack. Use material you own or have permission to use. Keep confidential customer records, private conversations, payment details, and account passwords out of it. The pack should answer these questions:

  1. Who do you help? Describe the customer’s situation in ordinary language. “Owners who lose track of incoming service inquiries” is more useful than “everyone who wants success.”
  2. What do you actually offer? List services, products, service areas, business hours, prices you are willing to publish, exclusions, and a working booking or purchase URL.
  3. What can you substantiate? Include approved explanations, current policy pages, and customer examples you have permission to publish. Label hypothetical examples clearly.
  4. How should you sound? Supply three short pieces of your own writing. Specify words to avoid and the level of formality. Ask for clear, specific language.
  5. What questions can be answered automatically? Start with published hours, service areas, how to obtain a resource, and the steps to request help.
  6. What requires a person? Include account-specific questions, refunds, complaints, negotiations, professional advice, and information that changes in real time.

Assign a date and an owner to facts that can change. For example: “Consultation booking link—checked October 31, 2026—owner: office manager.” If you do not publish prices, write “Do not quote a price; direct the person to the approved inquiry form.” That is a usable instruction.

03 / Build a campaign

Turn one useful customer question into a connected week of content.

Choose an outcome you can observe: a resource request, a qualified inquiry, a booking, or a purchase. “Increase engagement” is too broad to tell you what to write. Pick one question that arises before that outcome and answer it well.

Illustrative example: a service business helps customers understand a complicated process. Its campaign offers a checklist called “What to prepare before your first consultation.” This is a fictional teaching example, not a claim about a particular business or its results.

1 · ExplainThree items that make a first conversation more productive. Explain why each matters.Offer the checklist.
2 · DemonstrateShow a blank example of an organized preparation sheet. Use fictional details.Invite the reader to save it.
3 · ClarifyAnswer one real frequently asked question using an approved source.Link to the relevant explanation.

This is an example sequence, not a required daily posting frequency. Use the number of posts you can make useful. On Instagram, a clear “Comment GUIDE and I’ll send the checklist” invitation fits the specific automated journey we build later. Use a normal link or another platform-appropriate route elsewhere; do not assume the Instagram trigger exists on another network.

Review the draft as the business owner

Read each post aloud. Check every price, link, date, claim, service area, and promised response. Remove unsupported superlatives. Replace generic opening sentences with the actual customer question. A useful post should still contain useful information if its sales invitation is removed.

For visuals, provide original photographs, approved screenshots, or clearly illustrative artwork. Check generated images for invented interface controls, credentials, testimonials, logos, and misleading before-and-after results. Write alt text describing what the real image shows. Keep essential text readable on a phone.

Download the campaign CSV below and keep a stable ID such as PREP-01-IG for each post. Record the approved version and destination record ID. If a connection times out, those identifiers help you check whether the post already exists before creating another.

04 / Connect publishing

Set up Buffer and prove that one draft reaches the right account.

Start on a desktop browser. Sign in to the social account you intend to use in another tab, especially if you manage both personal and business identities. For Facebook, use a Page you are authorized to manage. Buffer does not connect personal Facebook profiles for posting.

  1. In Buffer, choose + New → Connect a New Channel. Select the network and complete its permission prompts.
  2. Open Publish, select the connected channel, and use the cog beside its name to check the time zone and posting times. Repeat for each channel.
  3. Select + New Post → + Channels. Choose one destination for the first test.
  4. Add one approved caption and upload the intended media. For a later multi-channel post, use Customize for each network to adapt the versions.
  5. Save a draft. Open Drafts and verify the account, caption, image, and link.

Documented interface: connect channels and create and schedule posts. Labels can differ between web and mobile.

Checkpoint: a real, unscheduled draft exists in the correct Buffer channel. You can open it in Buffer without relying on the AI conversation.

Use this direct route whenever it is the easiest way to upload media or inspect a preview. Buffer’s own AI Assistant can refine text, but its current help page says it cannot read a URL you paste into a prompt. Supply the relevant factual text instead. Read the AI Assistant instructions.

05 / Work through chat

Connect ChatGPT to Buffer with the official connection.

MCP is simply a standard that lets an AI assistant use tools in another service. In this case, the official Buffer server provides actions for your Buffer account. OAuth means you authorize access on the service’s sign-in screen; you do not paste your password into the chat.

Check your account before paying for anything

The current OpenAI guide documents custom MCP plugins with read and write tools, subject to permissions. Older help and vendor setup pages still describe different interfaces and plan restrictions. We cannot establish one universal plan entitlement from these sources. Use the connection only if your account exposes it and permits the required draft action. A Buffer upgrade does not grant missing ChatGPT permissions. The direct Buffer workflow above remains fully usable.

  1. In ChatGPT on the web, open Plugins → + → Add custom MCP server.
  2. Name it Buffer. Enter https://mcp.buffer.com/mcp as the server URL and choose OAuth.
  3. Review the connection information and warning, then select Create as a plugin. Complete Buffer’s sign-in and authorization when prompted.
  4. Review the discovered tools and install the plugin. Start a chat, type @, and select Buffer.

If your interface instead shows Settings → Apps/Connectors → Advanced settings → Developer mode, Buffer still documents that older route. Do not keep searching for a missing toggle in an interface that uses Plugins. Your workspace administrator may control access.

Current setup: OpenAI’s custom MCP guide. Vendor endpoint and OAuth: Buffer’s ChatGPT guide.

06 / Approve and schedule

Move reviewed content into the queue—and distinguish scheduled from published.

You can authorize a batch after reviewing it. Keep the authorization concrete: approved post IDs, final text and media, named channels, dates, time zones, and permitted actions. This makes delegation practical without requiring a separate discussion about every keystroke.

Copy: schedule only the approved drafts

@Buffer Schedule ONLY the existing approved drafts listed below.
Post ID | exact channel | date | local time | time zone
[fill in each row]

Use the saved captions and media without rewriting them.
Check that each time is in the future and resolves to the intended
local time, including daylight-saving changes.
Use automatic publication only where the account and format support it.
If a manual reminder is required, identify that post and stop for review.
Do not create replacement copies, publish immediately, or alter other posts.
Return each saved post ID, channel, status, and scheduled timestamp.
If a request times out, check the existing record before retrying.

In Buffer’s own composer, choose Set Date and Time for a specific schedule, or use a queue option such as Next Available, then Schedule Post. Choosing Now publishes immediately. Review the queue or calendar after saving. Scheduling reference.

Automatic publishing and reminders are different

For supported accounts and formats, Buffer submits the post automatically. With notification publishing, Buffer sends a phone reminder and you finish in the social app. A reminder post is therefore unfinished until someone completes it. Some native creative options require that route; check the actual format instead of assuming every Reel, Story, music choice, or account behaves the same way. Buffer marks reminder posts with a phone icon. How notification publishing works.

07 / Build the conversation

Create an Instagram “comment GUIDE” workflow in Manychat.

This workflow gives someone the resource they requested. It uses a fixed, approved message sequence, so you can get reliable delivery working before adding an AI FAQ assistant.

Connect the right Instagram account

You need an Instagram Business or Creator account. In Manychat, use Settings → Instagram → Connect. Its current connection guide offers several routes: direct Instagram, Meta, and Meta Business Suite. Their prerequisites differ. The Meta Business Suite route requires the linked Facebook Page and suitable control of it; a direct Instagram connection does not universally require a Facebook Page. Choose the route available for your account and needed features, then authorize the correct identity. Official connection requirements.

Prepare a real public guide URL, a published post you own for controlled testing, and a separate test account. The guide must open for someone who is not signed into your business account. If the campaign post is still scheduled, it is not yet the published post you will select here.

Configure the trigger and the first two messages

  1. Open Automation → + New Automation → + Start From Scratch → Start with a blank.
  2. Select +New Trigger → Instagram → User comments on your Post or Reel.
  3. Choose Specific post or reel, select the intended published item, and click Continue.
  4. Include the keyword GUIDE. Continue, optionally add a reviewed public reply, then Save.
  5. Add an Instagram Send Message node and enable Send as a Private Reply. Use one opening text block with a Quick Reply labeled “Send the guide.”
  6. Connect that quick reply to a separate message containing your guide URL. Do not add automatic Next Steps to the opening private-reply node.
  7. Use Preview and its Instagram option. When ready for the controlled live test, select Set Live; later edits use Update.

Why the quick reply matters

The first comment-triggered private message does not, by itself, open the ordinary messaging window. A qualifying interaction such as a reply or quick reply does; an Open website click alone does not. Keep automated follow-up within the permitted window—Manychat’s documentation describes 24 hours after the relevant interaction for Instagram and Messenger. Do not use a human-support exception as permission for automated promotional follow-ups. Messaging-window reference.

A resource request also does not automatically authorize email marketing. If you offer a separate email subscription, explain what the person will receive and collect the appropriate permission. The resource-delivery example works without collecting an email address.

Checkpoint: the tester comments GUIDE, receives one opening DM, taps “Send the guide,” and receives the correct working link. Without that tap or another qualifying reply, your example does not send its follow-up.

Testing detail: Manychat’s trigger runs on a person’s first comment on that post; repeated comments from the same person will not reliably retest it. Use fresh controlled testers or a different test post. A typed reply can open the messaging window, but this example’s configured “Send the guide” quick reply advances its delivery branch. When the real campaign post publishes, select that actual post in the trigger and test the connection again. Scheduling a post in Buffer does not automatically bind its ID to your Manychat flow.

09 / Make stopping work

Configure a human handoff and a way to pause the conversation.

A fallback sentence is useful only if the request reaches someone. Name an actual monitored inbox or person. Promise a response time only if the business can meet it. For sensitive or regulated services, keep the automation focused on public information and routing; move individual cases into the business’s approved support process.

In Manychat’s Flow Builder, an Action node can use Automation → Pause all automations, including a Forever duration. Assign Conversation and Notify Assignees can be in that same node and run before the pause. The pause is terminal: do not put a required message after it. A step already executing can finish; pausing does not recall a message. Pause-action reference.

Build an explicit HELP command

  1. Open Automation → Keywords → + New Keyword. Enter HELP, select Instagram, and choose Message is for an exact command match.
  2. Select Create and attach the handoff automation. Send the acknowledgment below before the final Action node.
  3. In that node, configure the monitored assignee, notification, and Pause all automations → Forever. Enable the trigger and publish the automation.
  4. Test HELP from an external account. Verify the actual assignment, notification, and pause; document who can resume the conversation.

Keyword setup: Manychat’s Keywords trigger. Use a configured keyword for this control rather than relying on the AI to infer every request correctly.

Preserve and test the existing opt-out

Manychat documents default Stop and Unsubscribe opt-out automations for Instagram. Inspect them under Settings → Instagram; use Edit and Update if you change the content. Preserve their opt-out behavior instead of creating a competing STOP rule. Test both commands and the combined AI/custom-flow behavior. The explicit HELP pause remains a separate human-support control. Default opt-in and opt-out automations.

When something does not work

ChatGPT says it is connected, but it cannot create a Buffer draft.

Check whether the installed connection exposes a write action and whether your account or workspace permits it. Reading channels does not prove write access. Confirm that you selected Buffer for the message. Use the Buffer composer for the prepared content if the action remains unavailable; do not purchase another plan based on an unverified assumption.

The post is scheduled, but nothing appeared.

Inspect the scheduler’s actual status, the destination account, and its time zone. Check whether this was notification publishing and required completion on a phone. Then inspect the specific error and channel connection. Check the actual social profile before recreating the post.

The image appears in chat but not in Buffer.

A chat attachment may not be a file Buffer can fetch. Open the draft in Buffer and upload the approved asset there. If using an API media URL, confirm that the URL is reachable and remains available through publication; keep sensitive files private.

Commenting GUIDE does nothing.

Check the connected Instagram account, the published post selected in the trigger, the keyword rule, live status, and permissions. Use a fresh tester: the same person’s repeat comment on the same post is not a reliable retest. Check the tester’s message requests. Confirm that another trigger is not taking precedence.

The first DM arrives, but the guide does not.

Confirm that “Send the guide” is a Quick Reply wired to the delivery message. An Open website button does not create the same continuation. A typed reply may permit messaging but does not automatically follow this particular quick-reply branch. Inspect the actual flow and permitted window.

The customer gets two replies or an outdated answer.

Inspect overlapping Meta instant replies, other automations, the AI scenario, and default replies. Pause the conflicting component while diagnosing. Check all knowledge sources for the old fact; a new conversation may be needed to see a knowledge change reflected.

The AI invents prices or fails to hand off.

Disable the AI scenario while repairing the source material and routing. Keep the fixed resource-delivery flow only if it remains correct. Add an explicit approved pricing response, verify the configured assignee, and retest the HELP command. Resume AI only after the failed examples behave appropriately.

The automation was working, then stopped.

Check connection expiry, the platform’s status, plan or usage limits, changed post IDs, disabled triggers, and the messaging window. In Metricool, a disconnected account can return a Flow to Draft. Correct the specific cause and verify reactivation rather than repeatedly toggling unrelated settings.

Questions owners usually ask

Can I do the whole thing from ChatGPT?

ChatGPT can prepare the campaign and, with a compatible authorized connection, create or schedule posts. Some account setup, visual previews, media handling, and messaging controls are easier or necessary in the provider’s own interface. This guide uses chat where it is useful and gives every action a destination you can verify.

Will it keep posting when my laptop is closed?

Posts already saved in a cloud scheduler’s automatic publishing queue do not depend on you leaving the drafting chat open. Notification publishing still requires a person. A recurring AI task has its own execution requirements, especially if it depends on desktop files. Test the exact setup you chose.

Do I need to build a custom application or hire a developer?

The publishing and reply recipes here use existing tools and their documented interfaces. Custom development becomes relevant when you need an unsupported integration, unusual business logic, specialized security controls, or high-volume operational requirements. Prove the existing workflow before commissioning more software.

Can I use Claude or another AI assistant?

The source-pack, campaign, review, and analysis prompts are portable. Buffer documents connections for other assistants, including Claude. Follow the provider’s specific authentication and permission instructions and repeat the unscheduled-draft test. Do not assume another assistant has the same menu, account access, or background-task features. Buffer’s Claude integration.

Can the agent find trending topics and turn them into posts?

Yes, when it has a supported research tool, it can help investigate current questions. Give it your audience and geography, ask for dated primary sources, and require it to explain why the topic is useful to your customers. Treat search results as topic evidence, not a search-volume measurement. Feed the verified findings into the campaign prompt; preserve your own expertise and original examples.

How autonomous should I make it?

Authorize predictable actions only after the relevant tests pass. Resource delivery from a fixed approved flow is a different decision from an AI composing an unrestricted public answer. You can delegate a reviewed week of posts at once while retaining human decisions for new claims, account-specific problems, spending, and policy exceptions.
