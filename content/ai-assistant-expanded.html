Choose one assistant for a job you can verify.

For a solo owner starting with drafts, summaries or product information, try the free version of ChatGPT or Claude on one real task. If most of your work already lives in Gmail and Google Docs, first check whether your account has the Gemini features you need. If the main job is finding current public information, include Perplexity in the research trial. You do not need subscriptions to all four.

The right first purchase is the plan that removes a demonstrated limit in work you already value. That might be more file capacity, a required tool, or a shared workspace with administration. A higher price does not prove that the assistant understands your business or produces correct answers.

If you already have an assistant that handles the job well, include it as the incumbent. The comparison should beat your current way of working, including the time spent checking and fixing the result.

Separate the answer, the action and the background process.

An assistant can help you produce a customer email, organize an article or inspect a table. Those outputs are useful, but they are different from sending the email, changing your CRM or running every morning. Ask which of these three things you need:

Three jobs that are often marketed together
JobExampleWhat must exist
Create an answer or artifactDraft a reply from approved service facts.Relevant context, the requested output and a review step.
Take an action in an appCreate an unsent email draft or update a record.A supported connection, the right permission and a check in the destination.
Run on a trigger or schedulePrepare a lead summary each morning.A documented trigger, available integration, failure handling and an owner.

Writing “you are my assistant” in a prompt does not establish an app connection or a schedule. Some products support these capabilities, but you must verify the actual action on your account. If a tool says it created a draft, open the destination and confirm the draft exists. For a recurring process, test a normal run and a missing-data case before relying on it.

For connected workflows, use the lead follow-up tutorial or the social media workflow. They identify the separate tools and checks required after the writing is done.

01 / Buy the right kind of access

Free, individual paid, business workspace or API?

Free access is enough to run a small trial. ChatGPT’s free plan includes capabilities such as search and file uploads, with tool-specific limits. Claude’s standard Projects are also available on Free, with a maximum of five projects. Do not buy a plan simply to create the fact brief in this article.

Sources: ChatGPT Free FAQ and Claude project availability. Current access and limits should be checked in your account.

Buy a workspace for the controls you need

When several people use business information, evaluate administration, sharing, access removal and data handling as actual requirements. A set of personal logins is not the same thing as a managed workspace. OpenAI states that it does not train on ChatGPT Business workspace data; its personal-account data controls are different. That distinction matters more than an assistant describing itself as “business-ready.”

02 / Make a useful first output

Write the business brief once, then ask for a defined result.

Choose a task that already happens: a product card, an inquiry reply, a newsletter teaser or an internal summary. Write down what a satisfactory result must contain before opening the assistant. “Make this more professional” is difficult to evaluate. “Write a 100-word product card that keeps the supplied measurements and makes no care claims” has a clear finish line.

03 / See complete results

Three business jobs, with usable inputs and outputs.

These are original fictional examples written for this guide. They demonstrate an acceptable result; they are not output-quality tests, customer case studies or claims that a particular assistant produced them. Choose the example nearest your work and replace the facts.

Seller: turn a product record into a clear product card

Copy: seller input

BUSINESS: Cedarline Goods (fictional)
TASK: A product card of no more than 100 words.
F1: One natural-color cotton canvas tote.
F2: Finished outside size: 38 × 42 cm.
F3: Black printed leaf design on the front.
F4: Price: $18 before shipping. Quantity available: 20.
F5: Preparation: 2–4 business days.
F6: Props in photographs are not included.
UNKNOWN: Load capacity, machine-wash suitability and delivery date.
NEXT ACTION: Check the current shipping options before ordering.
DO NOT CLAIM: Organic certification, a tested weight capacity,
waterproofing, machine washability, or guaranteed delivery.

Copy: seller output example

Cotton Canvas Tote with Black Leaf Print

A natural-color cotton canvas tote with a black leaf design on the
front. The finished outside dimensions are 38 × 42 cm.

You receive one tote; any photo props are not included. The price is
$18 before shipping. Preparation takes 2–4 business days, separate
from delivery time. Check the current shipping options before ordering.

Why it passes the example brief: dimensions remain outside measurements; preparation does not become an arrival guarantee; unknown washability and capacity are omitted. Stock stays in the selling system, where it can change, instead of becoming a stale claim in evergreen copy. For the actual listing and images, follow the complete Etsy workflow.

Service business: qualify an inquiry without promising a slot or price

Copy: service input

BUSINESS: Northline Window Care (fictional)
TASK: Reply to a new inquiry in fewer than 100 words.
F1: Service area: Riverton and Westbank.
F2: Service: exterior ground-floor window cleaning.
F3: Roof work and high-ladder work are not offered.
F4: Scope must be reviewed before a quote or appointment is confirmed.
INQUIRY: Can you clean my upstairs windows this Friday for $50?
UNKNOWN: Customer location, access, suitable scope and availability.
NEXT ACTION: Clarify whether the customer also needs ground-floor
windows cleaned and ask for the neighborhood.
DO NOT: Accept the proposed price, confirm Friday, or imply that
upstairs/high-ladder work is offered.

Copy: service output example

Thanks for getting in touch. We clean exterior ground-floor windows
in Riverton and Westbank; we do not offer high-ladder or roof work.

If you also need ground-floor windows cleaned, please tell us your
neighborhood and how many windows are involved. We can then check
whether the work fits our service. A price and appointment would be
confirmed only after we review the scope.

Why it passes: the customer’s proposed $50 and Friday are not treated as business facts. The reply sets the service boundary and requests the information needed for a next step. It does not imply a booking exists. Use the lead follow-up tutorial to track that next action.

Copy: creator output example

Subject: Three checks before you publish your next graphic

Before creating another design, open one you already use.

First, check whether its main message stands out from the supporting
text. Then inspect the contrast between the text and its background
at the size people will actually see. Finally, view it on a phone:
what becomes difficult to read?

Choose one issue and fix it. These checks give you a concrete editing
step; they do not establish accessibility compliance or promise a
sales result.

Why it passes: every teaching point comes from the supplied lesson and no invented statistic is added to make the opening more persuasive. For a repeatable delivery process, continue with the welcome email tutorial or social content workflow.

04 / Reuse what worked

Save the brief, instructions and approved output together.

A useful project should make the next task easier to start and easier to check. Name it after the work, such as “Product copy — approved facts,” rather than a broad job title. Keep one current brief and archive superseded offers clearly. Do not leave two contradictory price sheets without identifying which is current.

In ChatGPT Projects

  1. Select New project in the sidebar and name the work.
  2. Add the approved brief as a file or pasted text.
  3. Open the project’s … → Project settings and add your drafting and claim-check instructions.
  4. Start the task inside that project. Review and correct the result.
  5. For an approved response you want to reuse, open its message menu and choose Add to project sources.

Project instructions apply within the project. Availability, file limits and sharing depend on the plan and workspace. Keep the brief current; a saved source does not independently verify its contents.

Source: Projects in ChatGPT. For a fuller setup, use our business-brief Project tutorial.

In Claude’s standard Projects interface

Open Projects → + New Project, name it, and add the brief with the knowledge-area + control. Use Set project instructions → Save instructions for the rules. Put approved reusable information into project knowledge; do not assume that a separate conversation automatically contributes all its context. Claude is rolling out a new Projects beta, so your interface may differ from this standard route.

Sources: standard project setup and current rollout notes.

If your work stays in Google Docs or Gmail

With eligible access, look for Ask Gemini in the Workspace app. Keep the current brief in an approved document, then ask for the specific draft or summary. Check the referenced material rather than assuming the assistant can see every shared file or delegated mailbox. Your administrator and content owners can restrict access.

Sources: using Gemini in Workspace and data-access controls.

Check the account’s data settings

For a personal ChatGPT account, OpenAI documents Settings → Data controls → Improve the model for everyone. Turning that setting off does not delete your chat history. Use your organization’s approved workspace and sharing rules for internal business material; check the relevant vendor’s controls rather than assuming every plan works the same way.

05 / Compare with evidence

Run five tasks and measure the work left for you.

Choose two candidates at most. Use the same non-sensitive inputs, output requirements and pass criteria. Record the date, plan and model or mode when visible. Save the raw response before editing. The following is a trial protocol you can perform; the result cells are intentionally blank because no tests have been claimed here.

Copy: controlled inputs for tasks 2, 3 and 5

TASK 2 — EXCEPTION
Fictional policy: We install standard shelving in Riverton.
Exception: We do not install into tile or natural-stone walls.
Request: Write a two-sentence service summary.
Expected check: The wall-surface exception must remain explicit.

TASK 3 — CONFLICT
Old record, March 1: Starter package costs $49 and includes two files.
Approved current record, November 1: Starter package costs $59 and
includes three files. This record replaces the March offer.
Request: State the current price and contents; identify any conflict.
Expected check: $59 and three files; March record identified as old.

TASK 5 — NUMBERS
Three items at $18 each; two items at $12 each; shipping $6.
No discounts are supplied. Tax and seller fees are not supplied.
Request: Show item subtotal and total before tax, and name exclusions.
Expected check: 3×18 + 2×12 = $78; $78 + $6 = $84 before tax.
Do not invent tax, fees, profit or a discount.

Define a critical error before starting: for example, an invented price, an excluded service presented as available, or a false policy promise. If one appears, the task fails that trial even if the writing sounds excellent. Correct it, revise the brief if needed and retest. A model’s self-reported confidence is not a substitute for this check.

Measure both the first response and the time required to make it acceptable. Include prompting, reading, source verification and correction. Compare that total with doing a similar task without the assistant. Repeat the important cases on another day or with different inputs before treating the workflow as dependable. Alternating the order of the tools can reduce the advantage gained from practice.

Specific problems and useful next steps
What happensWhat to do
The assistant promises stock, a price or an appointment it cannot confirm.Remove the promise, supply the current approved record and retest the same case.

Common buying questions

Which AI assistant is best for a small business?

There is no single winner established by this guide. Start with the job-based shortlist, then run the same factual, exception and source-check tasks in two candidates. Existing app access, verified output quality and correction time should decide the purchase.

How much does an AI business assistant cost?

Free plans can cover a small trial. The individual examples here are ChatGPT Plus at $20 monthly and Claude Pro at $20 monthly or $200 upfront annually. Workspace minimums, extra usage and app automation can make the full cost higher; use the scoped pricing table rather than a single starting price.

Can it act as my personal assistant and run the business?

It can help with defined work. Actions and background execution depend on actual product features, connections, permissions and triggers. Test the specific job, verify the destination result and keep an owner for exceptions.

Do I need to build a custom GPT?

No. A saved brief and a Project are enough for this workflow. OpenAI’s current Free FAQ says new GPT creation is unavailable on personal Free, Go, Plus and Pro accounts; eligible managed workspaces have separate permissions. Check the current account controls before planning around that feature.

Source: OpenAI Free FAQ.
