The difference in plain terms

Auto-attendant vs AI receptionist: routing a call and handling a request are different jobs.

A traditional auto-attendant follows a menu or routing rule: press a key, say a supported choice, reach an extension or mailbox. An AI receptionist can interpret a spoken request, ask intake questions, answer from approved information, and use supported actions such as booking. A human answering service supplies people to answer under your instructions. Voicemail records a message for later review.

Those labels overlap in marketing. RingCentral, for example, includes conversational AI in its broader auto-attendant family. Ask what the exact plan can do, which systems it can use, and what happens when it cannot finish. A realistic demonstration is more useful than the word “AI.”

Definitions and product scope: RingCentral auto-attendants, Microsoft auto-attendant configuration, Ruby’s human service, and Panasonic’s voicemail explanation. The decision criteria below are our analysis.

For an owner who wants fewer interruptions: start with routine intake and one organized callback queue. Add booking only for appointments that need no further qualification. Route exceptions to a staffed person or a clearly owned callback; do not send every routine call to your mobile.

A worked month: 40 calls, with a clear scope

Assume 40 billable calls, no other charges, and a configured Extra Calls allowance. For an intake-only Smith.ai Free setup, the arithmetic is (40 − 25) × $3 = $45. If 10 of those calls also use a $3 paid feature, the modeled subtotal becomes $45 + (10 × $3) = $75. A required paid calendar and the underlying phone service remain additional costs; their prices are not included in that subtotal.

For a human-service comparison, assume those same 40 calls consume exactly two billable receptionist minutes each: 40 × 2 = 80 minutes. Ruby’s listed 100-minute tier is $395/month. That tier covers the assumed minutes; we have not priced a smaller tier plus overages. These are different services and billing units, so the difference is not evidence of equivalent results or guaranteed savings.

Your break-even check: divide the complete monthly cost by the contribution from one genuinely additional completed job. At an illustrative $80 contribution per job, a $75 subtotal takes one additional job to cover; a $395 cost takes five. Add phone, calendar, review time, and other fees first. An answered call is not automatically an additional sale.

03 / Start with the routing you already have

Keep voicemail when a dependable callback is enough; keep a menu when it saves a transfer.

Check a recent sample of missed calls before adding a subscription. Categorize them as simple questions, new inquiries, existing-customer requests, unwanted calls, and exceptions. Record whether each needed a live conversation or could wait. This is your own operating baseline, not a vendor’s missed-revenue estimate.

For voicemail, name the business, ask for the minimum useful details, and state a callback expectation you can meet. For a menu, give each choice a distinct destination. An example is “new service requests,” “existing appointments,” and “other questions.” Define what happens when no key is pressed and when the destination does not answer.

04 / Give the receptionist approved facts

Configure a narrow intake before connecting the calendar.

  1. Check the business profile. In Smith.ai, review the business name, website, address, and opening hours. Older documentation calls this area Business Info; newer screens use Business profile. Correct the values rather than assuming the website import is complete. The business-information guide says saved changes apply immediately.
  2. Add a small FAQ set. Open the profile’s FAQ editor and add precise answers about services, coverage, prices, and preparation. Smith.ai documents a 50-question total, including accepted suggestions. Review new suggested answers before approving them. A suggestion is not an authorized change to your business policy.
  3. Separate caller types. Use the caller-instructions configuration for new inquiries, existing customers, and the default route. Specify the details to collect and the permitted next action for each. A new inquiry should not inherit an existing-customer promise or a refund decision.
  4. Check the notification destination. Decide where summaries will be reviewed and who owns unresolved requests. In your first version, a consistently reviewed call list is preferable to adding several integrations nobody monitors.

Vendor controls: Business information · FAQ editing and limits · Caller-type settings. The operating boundaries are our implementation recommendations.

Expected output: an intake route that can accurately record a request and identify its next owner without making a booking. Test that simpler route first. It remains your fallback if calendar access later fails.

05 / Connect one booking that the business can honor

Prepare Calendly, then connect and activate the correct event.

In Calendly, open Scheduling and edit the specific event. For our example, set a 15-minute phone discussion, the correct host and time zone, real availability, and enough notice to prepare. Use the editor’s buffer and meeting-limit controls where needed. Keep the form short: do not ask the caller to repeat information without a business reason. These durations and rules are our example, not vendor defaults.

Calendly’s event controls and buffer behavior. For a full booking journey, see our self-service booking tutorial.

  1. Verify the paid requirements. Smith.ai’s Calendly integration requires a paid Calendly plan. Confirm Smith.ai’s booking-feature charge and enabled billing settings as well; a supported integration is not proof of a zero-cost workflow.
  2. Connect the accounts. In Smith.ai, open Integrations, select Calendly from the native options, and use its connection control. Sign in to the intended Calendly account and authorize the connection. Confirm that it appears as connected.
  3. Choose the eligible caller type. In the instructions area, open that caller type’s configuration. Select the appointment action, choose Calendly as the scheduling service, and enter the public event link. Save the change only when the intake rules and event are ready.
  4. Use one event link. The documented integration supports one event type per caller type. Use the direct event page, not your general Calendly page, a page with several choices, or a single-use link. Duration and availability come from that event’s settings.

Two separate setup tasks: connect Calendly, then enable scheduling for the caller type.

Expected output: a controlled test creates a real event in the intended calendar and the caller receives the intended confirmation. A connected badge, a plausible transcript, or an offered time does not establish that the appointment exists.

Using a different calendar? Smith.ai also documents a live-agent route using a publicly accessible booking page. Selecting that route involves a human scheduling service at the stated $3-per-call add-on, rather than the native Calendly action. Test that your page loads without a private login and that the selected service matches your budget.

06 / Make the human route real

“Your team” and a paid receptionist are different destinations.

Smith.ai’s documented escalation controls let you choose your own team or its live-agent network when a caller asks for a human. Configure that choice in call handling, select the intended destination, and save. Check the availability of your team member and the outcome if the call is unanswered.

The live-agent billing guide charges $3 for a qualifying call involving its human add-on, once per call even if there are several live-agent interactions. It distinguishes transfers to your business from those add-ons. That distinction does not remove your own phone provider’s fees or make your team available after hours.

Which human interactions are billable.

Our handoff rule: require either an answered transfer or a callback record with an owner. If neither exists, the request remains unresolved. A friendly closing statement cannot substitute for the missing destination.

07 / Prove the route before forwarding customers

A simulated conversation does not prove that booking or transfer works.

In Smith.ai’s Quality Studio, create a goal and add a scenario describing what the caller says and what the receptionist must achieve. Start with “capture an accurate request,” then add “book an eligible discussion” and “complete the fallback.” These are evaluation scenarios for you to run, not tests we claim to have completed.

Creating goals and scenarios.

Use Run Simulation to inspect the proposed conversation. The vendor says simulations stop at a booking or transfer handoff and cannot check real calendar availability. Then use Make a Test Call in your own account to exercise the voice and handoff path with destinations you control. Check the real calendar and receiving device afterward. Free includes separate monthly allowances of 25 test calls and 50 simulations; those are separate from 25 real calls.

09 / Keep the useful work, remove the waste

Measure correct outcomes and interruptions.

For each reviewed call, record: reason, correct contact details, authorized answer, booking or handoff result, owner, resolution, and billable usage. Count duplicate records, invented commitments, and unresolved requests as defects even if the system’s answer rate looks good. Review the transcript and destination records when a score disagrees with what actually happened.

Compare one operating period with your earlier baseline. Did callbacks get completed? Did eligible bookings appear correctly? Did the owner receive fewer routine interruptions? Did the actual invoice fit the budget? Expand only the routes that work. A service that records five useful requests accurately may be more suitable for your business than a more ambitious configuration that creates work to repair.

Common questions

What is the difference between an auto-attendant and an AI receptionist?

A traditional auto-attendant follows predefined routing choices. An AI receptionist can interpret the caller’s request and perform supported conversational tasks. Some vendors use “AI auto-attendant” for overlapping products; compare the actual permitted actions and integrations.

Is an answering service better than voicemail?

It fits when a conversation needs to happen before you can respond. Voicemail can be enough when callers can wait and you reliably return messages. The service still needs your instructions and an available escalation destination; it cannot supply your business judgment automatically.

Can I keep my current number?

Check whether your existing provider supports forwarding the specific calls you want covered, and whether charges or restrictions apply. Forwarding and porting are different changes. Test a reversible forwarding route before considering a number move.

Can an AI receptionist book appointments for free?

Do not infer that from a feature checklist. The Smith.ai example has paid booking conditions on Free, and native Calendly integration requires a paid Calendly plan. Verify both sides of the connection and the account’s enabled billing settings.

Do I need to replace the auto-attendant?

No. A schedule or menu can remain in front of the receptionist when it serves a purpose. Keep the route short, test the combined journey, and prevent a fallback from returning to the same starting point.
